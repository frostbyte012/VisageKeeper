\section{Comparison with Earlier Works}

What matters for a defense is not how well it scores on clean faces, but
what is left once the attacker knows it is there. Table~\ref{tab:baselines}
tests every method on LFW against the same adaptive attacker. Feature
squeezing tells the story. On clean faces it reaches $94.9\%$, and against
a simple fixed attack it even beats the undefended model ($31.8$ vs
$20.0$). Then the attacker adapts to it, and it drops to $0.0$. Every
other purification defense does the same, including our own frequency
filter: strong at first, then nothing.

\textit{VisageKeeper} is the only one left standing, holding $25.8\%$
where the other five all reach zero, and it returns a proof instead of a
score. This costs us: clean accuracy falls from $97.1\%$ to $91.8\%$, and
the protection is uneven, stopping fake matches about half the time but
not hiding. The denoiser is what makes it work. Noise alone scores $49.5$,
but only because it starts at a coin flip ($52.7\%$) and has nothing left
to lose.
